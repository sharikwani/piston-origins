Why the Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 Was Banned in the US

The car Americans could only drive in a video game

For 25 years, most Americans could only drive the Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 in a video game. Import one the wrong way and the government could take it away. Today it is one of the most wanted cars on Earth, and the reason starts long before the R34 itself, with the car that first carried the GT-R badge.

The Hakosuka: where the Skyline GT-R began

The story starts in 1969. Prince had just merged with Nissan, and its engineers dropped a racing six into a boxy family saloon. They called it the GT-R, for Gran Turismo Racing. Officially it was the Skyline 2000GT-R, chassis code PGC10, but fans called it the Hakosuka, the "box Skyline", and it dominated Japanese racing.

Then the oil crisis hit. The second-generation GT-R lasted for just 197 cars, and the name went quiet for 16 years.

The R32 GT-R and the Godzilla nickname

In 1989 the GT-R came back with a vengeance. The R32 GT-R had a twin-turbo six and clever all-wheel drive, and it crushed everything on the track. In Australia it won so much in touring-car racing that Wheels magazine gave it a nickname: Godzilla, the monster from Japan. So one of the best-known names in Japanese performance cars was, in fact, an Australian idea.

The R34 GT-R reveal: shorter, stiffer, sharper

On 8 January 1999 Nissan revealed the R34. The brief can be summed up in three words: shorter, stiffer, sharper. The body was around 56 per cent stiffer than the R33's, and the wheelbase was cut by 55 mm.

What made the R34 feel so far ahead of its time was the technology packed around the driver. It used ATTESA E-TS Pro all-wheel drive with an active differential, Super-HICAS four-wheel steering and a Getrag six-speed gearbox. In the dash sat a 5.8-inch multi-function display showing boost and temperatures, in 1999. The R34 was a computer on wheels, and it felt as if it had driven straight out of Gran Turismo.

The RB26DETT engine and the 276 hp gentlemen's agreement

Under the bonnet sat the legendary RB26DETT: 2.6 litres, six cylinders in a row and two turbochargers. Officially it made 276 hp (280 PS), the figure set by the same gentlemen's agreement between Japanese car makers that capped the Toyota Supra. Tested cars, however, made closer to 330. And tuners took the RB26 way, way past that, which is a large part of why the engine's reputation has outlived the car's production run.

Why the R34 GT-R couldn't be sold in the US

Gran Turismo is exactly where most Americans met the R34, because in America you couldn't buy one. The car was never built to US federal safety rules, the FMVSS standards, so it could not be sold or imported new. One California importer, Motorex, found a workaround in the early 2000s, until regulators shut it down.

That left a whole generation to fall in love with the car on a PlayStation, and in 2 Fast 2 Furious, where Paul Walker drove a silver and blue R34. The forbidden-fruit status only made the Skyline more desirable: a car you could race every evening on a console, but never park on your own drive.

Production numbers, the V-Spec II Nür and the $1.357 million auction

Only 11,578 R34 GT-Rs were built before production ended in 2002. The rarest of them, the Nür editions such as the V-Spec II Nür, number only in the hundreds.

That scarcity, combined with the car's film history, has pushed prices to extraordinary levels. In 2023 an R34 from the Fast and Furious films, customised for Paul Walker, sold at auction for $1.357 million. More than a million dollars, for a Skyline.

The 25-year rule: Godzilla comes to America

After all that waiting, the R34 is finally becoming legal in America. Under the 25-year import rule, each car becomes eligible 25 years after the month it was built. The first, early-1999 cars crossed that line in 2024, and because production ran until 2002, more R34s become eligible as each build month passes.

For the generation that grew up with it on a screen, that means the car regulators once kept out can now arrive legally, one build month at a time. Godzilla is coming to America at last.

Quick answers

Why was the Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 banned in the USA?

It was never certified to US federal safety and emissions standards (FMVSS), so it could not be legally sold or imported new. Apart from a small number brought in through the Motorex loophole, Americans had to wait for the 25-year import rule.

Is the R34 GT-R legal in the US?

Under the 25-year rule, each R34 becomes eligible for import 25 years after its build month. The first cars (early 1999 builds) became eligible in 2024, and production ran to 2002.

How many R34 GT-Rs were made?

11,578, between 1999 and 2002.

Why is the Nissan GT-R called Godzilla?

Australia's Wheels magazine nicknamed the R32 GT-R "Godzilla", the monster from Japan, after it dominated Australian touring-car racing.
