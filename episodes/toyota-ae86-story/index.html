The AE86 Wasn't Fast. So Why Is It a Legend?

12 October 2026 · 5:08 · 170 views on YouTube

The full Toyota AE86 history: how a slow, cheap 1983 Corolla became the Hachi-Roku legend behind Initial D and drifting. It was never fast, so why does everyone still want one?

From Toyota keeping rear-wheel drive when the rest of the Corolla range went front-wheel drive, to the 4A-GE twin-cam that loved to rev, the $11,530 Corolla GT-S, Keiichi Tsuchiya "the Drift King", Initial D and the "Takumi tax", all the way to the 2012 Toyota 86, this is the full AE86 story.

Quick answers

Was the Toyota AE86 actually fast?

No. Car and Driver measured the Corolla GT-S at 9.5 seconds from 0 to 60 mph. Its legend comes from its light, rear-wheel-drive balance and the rev-happy 4A-GE engine, which made it ideal for drifting.

Why is the AE86 called Hachi-Roku?

Hachi-roku is Japanese for "eight-six", from the car's AE86 chassis code.

What is the "Takumi tax"?

The price premium on AE86s caused by Initial D, whose hero Takumi drives one. A Corolla GT-S that cost $11,530 new in 1985 sold for $40,000 at auction in 2021.
