Mazda's Rotary Engine Should Never Have Worked (RX-7 & 787B Story)

6 October 2026 · 6:24 · 235 views on YouTube

The Mazda rotary engine has no pistons. It drinks fuel, burns oil, and nearly bankrupted Mazda, yet it won Le Mans with the 787B and powered the RX-7. So why do people love the rotary so much?

From a 1961 license deal with NSU to the "devil's nail marks" on early engines, the Cosmo Sport and the 84-hour Nürburgring marathon, the 1973 oil crisis, the RX-7, the 787B's 1991 Le Mans win, the FD RX-7 and the rotary's 2023 comeback in the MX-30 R-EV, this is the full Mazda rotary story.

Quick answers

How does a rotary engine work without pistons?

A Wankel rotary engine uses a triangular rotor spinning inside an oval-like housing. As it turns, the three chambers between the rotor and the housing take in, compress, ignite and exhaust the fuel mixture, so there are no pistons, connecting rods or valves.

When did Mazda win Le Mans?

Mazda won the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1991 with the four-rotor 787B.

Is the Mazda rotary engine still made?

Yes. Mazda restarted rotary production in 2023, using it as a range-extending generator in the MX-30 R-EV.
