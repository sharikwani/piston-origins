Toyota Lost Money on Every Lexus LFA. Owners Didn't.

19 October 2026 · 4:22 · 131 views on YouTube

In 2010, Toyota sold the Lexus LFA for $375,000 and still lost money on every single one. Today one has sold for $1.81 million. Here's the full story of Toyota's craziest supercar.

The 4.8-litre V10 built with Yamaha revved from idle to 9,000 rpm in 0.6 seconds, so fast that a needle couldn't keep up and Lexus fitted a digital rev counter. Toyota threw away five years of work on an aluminium car and started again in carbon fibre, tuned the engine's sound like a musical instrument, chased the Nürburgring, and built only 500 cars by hand.

Quick answers

Did Toyota lose money on the Lexus LFA?

Yes. Toyota sold the Lexus LFA for $375,000 and still lost money on every one of the 500 cars it built by hand between 2010 and 2012. In April 2026 an LFA sold on Bring a Trailer for $1.81 million.

Why does the Lexus LFA have a digital rev counter?

Its 4.8-litre V10 could rev from idle to 9,000 rpm in 0.6 seconds, faster than a mechanical needle could follow, so Lexus fitted a digital rev counter.

Why did the Lexus LFA switch from aluminium to carbon fibre?

After about five years of development on an aluminium body, Toyota restarted the car in carbon-fibre-reinforced plastic to make it lighter and stiffer.
