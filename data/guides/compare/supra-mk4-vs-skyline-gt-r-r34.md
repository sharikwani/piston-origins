## Supra Mk4 vs R34 GT-R: two answers to the same question

The fourth-generation Toyota Supra (A80) and the Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 are the two Japanese performance cars most often set against each other. Both use twin-turbocharged inline-six engines, both were officially rated at 280 PS (about 276 hp) in Japan, and both became stars of the Fast and Furious films. Yet they were designed in different ways and for different jobs. The Supra was a light, rear-wheel-drive grand tourer that Toyota also sold in America; the R34 was a technology-packed, all-wheel-drive homologation special that was never sold new in the US.

They also overlap less in time than people assume. The A80 launched in 1993 and was sold in Japan until 2002, while the R34 GT-R arrived in January 1999 and also ended production in 2002. The episode pages on each car cover their histories; this page sets them side by side.

## Drivetrain: rear-wheel drive vs ATTESA all-wheel drive

The biggest difference between the two is how they put power down. The Supra Turbo is rear-wheel drive, with Toyota's six-speed V160 manual, developed with Getrag, or a four-speed automatic. Its engine, the 3.0-litre 2JZ-GTE, uses sequential turbochargers to give strong low-speed response.

The R34 GT-R uses ATTESA E-TS Pro all-wheel drive with an active rear differential, Super-HICAS four-wheel steering and a Getrag six-speed manual. Its 2.6-litre RB26DETT has parallel turbochargers and a racing pedigree from Group A. In practice, the R34 is the car built to deploy its power in all conditions and on a circuit, and the Supra is the simpler, more traditional sports car, which is part of why tuners like it as a base for big-power builds.

## Performance figures: Supra vs R34 0–60

On the official numbers, the Japanese-market cars were level at 280 PS. Export Supras were rated higher, at 320 hp and 315 lb-ft for the US Supra Turbo, while Nissan's own heritage records give the R34 V-spec II 280 PS at 6,800 rpm and 392 Nm (289 lb-ft) at 4,400 rpm. Many enthusiasts believe the R34's true output was higher; PistonHeads' buying guide puts it nearer 327 hp.

Measured acceleration was close. TopSpeed lists 4.6 seconds from 0 to 60 mph for the US Supra Turbo, the figure Car and Driver recorded in its 1993 test, and an electronically limited 155 mph. PistonHeads quotes 0 to 62 mph in 4.8 seconds for the R34 GT-R. The two figures use slightly different benchmarks, so they are best read as a dead heat on paper. PistonHeads' guide gives both 155 mph and 165 mph for the R34's top speed in different places, so treat that figure with caution.

## Supra vs R34 weight

The two cars weigh almost the same. TopSpeed lists the US Supra Turbo at 3,417 lb, about 1,550 kg, a figure helped by chief engineer Isao Tsuzuki's weight-saving programme. Nissan's Heritage Collection gives the R34 GT-R V-spec II a curb weight of 1,560 kg. The R34 carries all-wheel drive and four-wheel steering in roughly the same mass, while the Supra spends its weight on a bigger engine and a body with more space for two occupants and luggage.

## How many were made?

Neither car was common. The R34 GT-R's total production is widely given as 11,578 between 1999 and 2002. PistonHeads' breakdown of the series gives roughly 2,700 standard cars and 4,300 V-specs in the first series, then about 1,270 standard cars, 1,850 V-spec IIs, 740 V-spec II Nür models and 650 M-specs in the second, with the Nür versions totalling just over a thousand.

Toyota never published an equivalent breakdown, and Supra figures vary by source. Retail sales figures compiled by the enthusiast site MKIV.com show 11,239 A80 Supras sold in the US between 1993 and 1998, of which 6,977 were Turbos. Sales collapsed after 1995: the same table records just 88 US Turbos for 1996. Japanese-market production, which continued to 2002, is not included in those figures.

## Prices then and now

When new, the Supra Turbo was the cheaper car. Road & Track's 1994 Buyers Guide lists the Supra Turbo at $44,100, against $36,900 for the base car; TopSpeed's price table gives $39,900 for 1993, so the exact figure depends on year and source. The R34 GT-R was never priced in the US, but PistonHeads gives a UK price new of £54,000 for an imported car.

Values have since climbed far beyond those figures. In January 2019 a 7,111-mile 1994 Supra Turbo sold on Bring a Trailer for $121,000, which Jalopnik suggested might be the most a used Supra had fetched outside the film cars. In June 2021 the orange "Stunt 1" Supra from The Fast and the Furious, an automatic, sold at Barrett-Jackson's Las Vegas auction for $550,000, Car Throttle reported. For the R34, PistonHeads reported in March 2020 that the best UK cars went for more than £100,000. In May 2023 the Bayside Blue R34 driven by Paul Walker in Fast and Furious sold through Bonhams for $1,357,000 including premium, more than doubling the previous Skyline GT-R auction record of $577,500 set the year before, according to JUST CARS. Film cars are outliers, so they say more about fame than about the value of an ordinary example.

## Is the Supra Mk4 or R34 legal in the US?

The Supra was sold new in America from 1993 to 1998, so US-market cars are fully legal. Japanese-market Supras, including the RZ with its CT20 turbochargers, could only be imported once they qualified under the 25-year rule.

The R34 GT-R was never certified to the US Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, so it could not be sold or imported new. US law, at 49 U.S.C. 30112(b)(9), exempts any motor vehicle at least 25 years old, and the age is counted from the date the car was built. The first, early-1999 R34s became eligible in 2024, and the last cars, built in 2002, reach that age during 2027. Before that, a small number arrived through the importer Motorex until regulators stopped it, and Drifted notes that a few very rare variants, such as the Nismo Z-tune, could be approved under the separate Show or Display rule, which limits use to 2,500 miles a year. Individual states can add their own registration and emissions rules, so check locally before buying.

## Cultural status: film stars and video games

Both cars owe much of their fame to screens. The Supra was already respected by tuners before an orange A80 starred in The Fast and the Furious in 2001, and its 2JZ-GTE's tolerance for huge power kept it at the centre of tuning culture. Jalopnik, reporting the 2019 sale above, wondered aloud whether that popularity had created a price bubble.

The R34 took a different route. Because Americans could not buy one, a generation met it in Gran Turismo and in 2 Fast 2 Furious, and its forbidden-fruit status grew with every year it stayed out of reach. Its badge also carries the weight of the GT-R's racing history, from the Hakosuka to the R32 that earned the nickname Godzilla.

The fair verdict is that the Supra is the purer sports car and the easier base for a high-power build, while the R34 is the more advanced and rarer machine, with the higher values to match. For a buyer, the choice is less about which is faster and more about rear-wheel-drive simplicity against all-wheel-drive technology.
