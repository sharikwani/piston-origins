## What is the Nissan GT-R R35?

The R35 is the GT-R that stood on its own. Nissan dropped the Skyline name, built a four-seat coupé around a hand-assembled twin-turbo V6, a rear-mounted dual-clutch gearbox and all-wheel drive, and sold it for a fraction of the price of the cars it was measured against. Nissan announced the production car on 24 October 2007, and Japanese sales began on 6 December 2007, with North America following in June 2008 and other markets during 2008, according to webCG's launch report. The car was built at Nissan's Tochigi plant.

It then stayed in production for almost 18 years. Nissan's farewell release, dated 26 August 2025, says roughly 48,000 were built and that the last car was a Premium edition T-spec in Midnight Purple for a customer in Japan. Our episode covers the 2008 Nürburgring row with Porsche; this page is for anyone looking to understand or buy one.

## Nissan GT-R R35 specs

At launch the 3.8-litre VR38DETT made 480 PS at 6,400 rpm and 60.0 kgm (588 Nm) from 3,200 to 5,200 rpm, and Nissan quoted 0–100 km/h in 3.6 seconds (webCG). Drive goes through the GR6 six-speed dual-clutch transaxle, which MotoIQ notes made the GT-R the first production car with an all-wheel-drive dual-clutch transaxle. MotoIQ puts the car at about 4,000 lb, a reminder that this was never a lightweight.

Japanese prices ran from ¥7,770,000 to ¥8,347,500. In the US the 2009 base car was $69,850 and the Premium $71,900, before mid-year increases to $76,840 and $79,090. evo lists the first UK cars at £56,795.

## Nissan GT-R model-year changes, 2009 to 2025

Rather than one big facelift, the R35 changed every year, which matters when you compare cars. Nissan's farewell release says output rose from 480 PS at launch to 570 PS from the 2017 model year. evo's history, quoting UK figures in bhp, records a light facelift in 2011 with LED running lights, revised front styling and 523bhp; 542bhp and further suspension changes in 2012; new lights but no power gain in 2014; and the biggest update in 2017, with 562bhp, a chassis evo says was five per cent stiffer and a heavier, more upmarket cabin. More efficient turbochargers followed in 2019. PistonHeads adds that the 2017 car got a new grille, softer gearbox mapping and an improved interior.

Launch control also changed almost yearly. MotoIQ describes five versions between 2009 and 2013, with the early cars' launch settings blamed for broken gearboxes and a 2012 change to a gentler "R Mode Start". Service intervals changed too: PistonHeads says cars built up to the end of 2010 need servicing every six months or 6,000 miles, and later cars every 12 months or 12,000 miles.

European sales ended in March 2022 because of drive-by noise rules, PistonHeads reports. Nissan carried on for Japan and the US, and in January 2023 announced revised T-spec and Nismo models; evo notes that the 2024 facelift was never sold in Europe.

## GT-R trims and special editions: Black Edition, Track Edition, Nismo and T-spec

Most markets offered the car in base, Premium and Black Edition forms, with names varying by country; in the UK the 2017 range was Pure at £79,995, Recaro at £81,995 and Prestige at £82,495, according to PistonHeads. In the US the 2012 Black Edition cost $95,100.

The Track Edition sat between the road cars and the Nismo. PistonHeads lists it at £88,560 in the UK, "engineered by Nismo" with forged 20-inch wheels and some Nismo suspension and body parts but standard power. MotoIQ gives the 2014 US price as $115,710.

The **GT-R Nismo** arrived in 2014. PistonHeads gives it 600hp and 481lb ft, a 196mph top speed, 410mm carbon-ceramic brakes and carbon body panels, at £120,000 rising to £125,000. Nissan says later Nismos used turbochargers derived from its GT3 racing car, and a Nismo holds the R35's best Nürburgring lap, 7:08.679 in November 2013 with Michael Krumm.

The **T-spec** began as a Japanese limited edition. evo reported in September 2021 that two versions, the Premium edition T-spec and the Track edition engineered by Nismo T-spec, would each be limited to 100 cars, both with carbon-ceramic brakes, a carbon rear wing and special badging; the Track version also had a carbon roof and bootlid. Midnight Purple and Millennium Jade, colours from earlier Skyline GT-Rs, returned to the range at the same time. The T-spec name stayed on through the final model years, which is why the very last R35 wears it.

## How the GT-R R35 was received

The press loved it. evo named it its 2008 Car of the Year, ahead of the Lamborghini Gallardo LP560-4, Porsche 997 GT2 and Renaultsport Mégane R26.R, writing that "the mighty Nissan GT-R cannot be denied its victory". The praise was not unconditional: PistonHeads recalls early cars as firm and noisy inside, and evo's Henry Catchpole felt in 2016 that splitting the range into separate models cost the car some of its all-round appeal.

## GT-R in motorsport

The R35 raced as well as lapping the Nordschleife. Nissan lists five Super GT GT500 wins and three GT300 wins, a 2013 Blancpain GT Series Pro-Am class win, victory in the 2015 Bathurst 12 Hour and five Super Taikyu wins. A Nismo also set a production-car lap record at Tsukuba, 59.078 seconds in January 2024.

## Nissan GT-R R35 common problems

The engine is not usually the worry. PistonHeads' 2014 powertrain guide calls the VR38DETT very reliable even when tuned, and evo says the standard engine can handle over 800bhp with supporting turbo upgrades. Its warnings are more specific: blue smoke on a cold start or as the turbos spool is a bad sign, and PistonHeads' 2023 guide says cam chains can become noisy from around 40,000 miles, especially after poor oil changes, with a full kit at roughly £2,000.

The GR6 gearbox is the part to research. MotoIQ describes overheating and limp mode after hot laps, broken first gears from aggressive launches, controller faults that lock the car into odd or even gears, and debris jamming the shift solenoids; it says a replacement can cost about $20,000 in labour. PistonHeads mentions faulty solenoids on early cars, chipped gears that whine, and collateral damage costing around £5,000. It also notes that Nissan refused some early warranty claims where launch control had been used, and that a specialist can read the ECU's launch count.

Almost every GT-R rattles at idle in neutral. PistonHeads explains that this bellhousing noise comes from a bearing moving in its case and is usually harmless. Other checks from the same guides: front discs that crack (up to £3,000 a set), mismatched tyres that upset the four-wheel-drive system, a small battery that goes flat in cold weather, soft paint on darker colours and a vulnerable undertray. evo adds ABS pump failure and worn seat bolsters, and says pre-2011 cars have more reported issues. Inspect tuned cars carefully and get a specialist inspection on any car.

## How much is a Nissan GT-R R35 worth?

Values depend heavily on year, version and modifications. In the UK, evo's guide from September 2025 put 2007–2012 cars from around £30,000, 2012–2016 cars from £40,000, 2017-on cars from £60,000 and used Nismos in the £130,000 range. PistonHeads in March 2023 said prices started at about £70,000 for early 2016-on cars.

In the US, Motorious reported in August 2026, using Hagerty data, that excellent base cars traded around $59,500 and that Hagerty valued a good 2018 Nismo at $130,000, with 14 public auction sales of that model in three years ranging from $86,741 to $216,004. The same article reported a 1,300-mile 2021 Nismo selling on Bring a Trailer for $312,500. Jalopnik, in February 2026, cited a low-mileage 2021 T-spec sale at $163,000. Hagerty's data, as Motorious reported it, suggests R35 values stopped falling once production ended.
