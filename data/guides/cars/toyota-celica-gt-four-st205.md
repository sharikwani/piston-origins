## What is the Toyota Celica GT-Four ST205?

The ST205 is the third and last Celica GT-Four, the turbocharged, four-wheel-drive Celica that Toyota built so it could go rallying in Group A. It was based on the sixth-generation Celica and went on sale in Japan on 2 February 1994, according to Toyota's own history. PistonHeads reports that official UK imports ended in December 1996 because of slow sales, while Japanese production continued, with a minor facelift in December 1997, until 1999.

Our episode tells how the ST205 rally car was caught with a turbo restrictor bypass in 1995. This page is about the road car: what it is, which versions exist and what to look for.

## Celica GT-Four ST205 specs

Every ST205 uses the 1,998 cc 3S-GTE twin-cam turbocharged four. Toyota rates the Japanese car at 255 PS at 6,000 rpm. European cars were rated lower: evo gives 239bhp at 5,600rpm and 224lb ft at 4,000rpm, and PistonHeads quotes the same 239hp for UK cars. Part of the gap is simply the difference between PS and bhp.

Toyota lists the ST205 at 4,420 mm long and 1,750 mm wide on a 2,535 mm wheelbase, with a kerb weight of 1,380 kg; evo's figure is 1,390 kg. Drive goes through a five-speed manual, which PistonHeads identifies as the E154, and permanent four-wheel drive. evo quotes 0–62mph in 6.3 seconds and 152mph in its specification panel, though its own text gives a quicker 5.9-second 0–60mph time.

The main engineering change from the previous car was the front suspension. Toyota describes the ST205 as the top model to adopt its newly developed Super Strut layout. evo lists the other changes as a larger rear wing, better cooling, an aluminium bonnet to save weight and a water spray for the intercooler.

## ST205 WRC edition: how many were made?

To race in Group A, Toyota had to build road versions of the rally car. evo says only 2,500 were needed for the ST205, half the number required for its predecessor. Toyota's history says 2,100 WRC-homologated GT-Fours were released for the Japanese market in February 1994, and PistonHeads gives 2,500 WRC Limited Edition cars in total, so the remainder went to other markets. evo says only about 300 cars came to Europe.

PistonHeads lists what the homologation car added: a taller rear wing, a different exhaust manifold and revised engine management, and provision for water injection and anti-lag, which were not activated at the factory. It also says the cars were finished in Super White II, the same colour as the works rally cars. As with any limited edition, confirm a claimed WRC edition from its paperwork rather than its paint and wing.

## ST165 and ST185: the earlier Celica GT-Fours

The GT-Four started with the ST165, which Toyota launched in Japan on 1 October 1986 with 185 PS and a kerb weight of 1,350 kg. evo dates its arrival in Europe to 1988. It gave Toyota its first World Rally Championship win in 1989 and Carlos Sainz the drivers' title in 1990.

The ST185 followed in September 1989 with 225 PS. Its homologation model, the GT-Four RC in Japan and the Carlos Sainz Limited Edition in Europe, added a water-to-air intercooler and a larger turbo. Toyota says 5,000 RCs were released in 1992, and evo says about 3,000 went to Europe, 440 of them to the UK. The ST185 was the most successful of the three in competition, winning three drivers' titles and two manufacturers' titles according to evo, including Didier Auriol's 1994 championship.

## How the ST205 was received

The ST205 arrived as the most expensive car in its class. PistonHeads notes it cost £29,235 in the UK in 1994, against £24,000 for a Ford Escort Cosworth and £19,995 for a Subaru Impreza Turbo, and calls it "simply too expensive" for most buyers. Reviewers praised the ride, steering feel, relatively little turbo lag and fuel economy of more than 30mpg on a long run, but criticised a cabin of mostly dark plastic.

evo's retrospective describes it as the "black sheep" of the GT-Fours: formidable, solid and well built, but wider and less thrilling than its rivals, and sometimes coolly received by the press when new.

## ST205 rally history in brief

The works car never matched the ST185's record. evo says it won once, on the 1995 Tour de Corse, before Toyota Team Europe was excluded from the 1995 championship and banned for 1996 after the restrictor device was found at Rally Catalunya. Max Mosley's verdict, which evo quotes, was that it was "the most sophisticated and ingenious device I have seen in 30 years of motorsport".

## Celica GT-Four ST205 common problems

Start with authenticity and the body. evo warns that lesser Celicas can be dressed up as GT-Fours, so check the chassis number and paperwork; it also says ST205s are harder to fake than earlier cars. Both evo and PistonHeads list rust in the sills and arches, and PistonHeads adds the chassis-mounted pipes. Check for accident damage through panel gaps and door fit, and look for dents in the aluminium bonnet, which PistonHeads says is expensive to replace. A wet passenger footwell can mean a failed heater matrix: the part is cheap, but PistonHeads says the dashboard has to come out.

The 3S-GTE is durable when standard. PistonHeads recommends a cambelt change every 63,000 miles or five years and checking oil and coolant for cross-contamination, the charge cooler for correct coolant level and circulation, and the radiator for cracks. Smoke can mean a tired turbo or worse. Both guides advise caution with tuned cars, and put the safe limit for a healthy standard engine at about 300bhp.

The ST205-specific worry is the Super Strut front suspension. PistonHeads says noises over rough ground or under braking usually point to worn "figure 8" camber links, and that a full set of arms and links costs more than £1,200 in parts alone; evo also puts repairs to a worn setup in four figures. Elsewhere, listen for knocking from the rear, usually a tired differential mount, and check the clutch, because a replacement normally means removing the engine. PistonHeads notes that ST205 brake calipers are hard to find and that the standard 215/50 R16 tyre size has limited choice.

Japanese imports, identifiable by their lack of headlamp washers, often have lower mileage and better bodywork, but evo says they may be worth slightly less than UK cars.

## How much is a Celica GT-Four ST205 worth?

Prices have risen sharply since the car was a cheap used buy. PistonHeads in August 2018 said projects started at about £3,000, sound cars at £5,000 and the best at around £10,000. By February 2025 evo was quoting £15,000 to £20,000 for a decent higher-mileage UK car with good MOT history and up to about £50,000 for a low-mileage, near-perfect example, although its specification panel still listed £7,000 to £10,000.

Recorded US sales give a sense of the American market. On Bring a Trailer, a Japanese-market 1994 homologation car with about 70,000 miles sold for $16,150 in October 2019, and a modified 1994 car with about 122,000 miles sold for $11,250 in June 2020, according to Hypercars' archive. On PCarMarket in August 2023, a 1994 Japanese-market car with about 153,000 miles was bid to $10,500; the listing does not confirm that it sold. Those results are now some years old, so check recent sales before relying on them.
