## What is the Lexus LFA?

The LFA is Lexus's first supercar: a front-engined, rear-wheel-drive two-seater with a carbon-fibre body and a 4.8-litre V10 developed with Yamaha. Toyota spent roughly a decade on it, rebuilt it in carbon fibre after years of aluminium development, and built 500 by hand. Our episode tells that story and the engine guide covers the V10. This page covers the car as a collector's purchase: the specifications, the Nürburgring Package, how it was received, what to check and what LFAs sell for now.

## Lexus LFA specs

PistonHeads' buying guide lists the key figures. The 4,805 cc, 72-degree V10 makes 552hp at 8,700rpm and 354lb ft at 6,800rpm; Toyota quotes 412 kW (560 PS). It revs to 9,000rpm, with a fuel cut at 9,500rpm. Drive goes to the rear wheels through a six-speed single-clutch automated manual, not a twin-clutch gearbox, mounted at the back as a transaxle.

PistonHeads gives a weight of 1,480 kg, a 0-62mph time of 3.7 seconds, 0-100mph in 8.2 seconds and a top speed of 202mph. Brakes are carbon-ceramic, with six-piston Brembo calipers at the front, and the dampers are adjustable KYB units. The wheels are 20 inches, with 265/35 front and 305/30 rear tyres. The carbon-fibre structure, PistonHeads notes, was about 100 kg lighter than the original aluminium design.

## How many Lexus LFAs were made?

Exactly 500. Lexus says production at LFA Works, a dedicated facility within the Motomachi plant in Aichi, began on 15 December 2010 and ran for two years. The last car, a white Nürburgring Package, was completed on 14 December 2012. Lexus describes a build rate of one car a day and says about 170 hand-picked craftsmen handled parts, assembly and paint. Each car's production number is hand-written on parts underneath, which PistonHeads recommends checking to confirm provenance.

To discourage speculation, Lexus sold the car on terms that let it buy cars back. PistonHeads reports that in the UK the LFA was sold only through Lexus's Park Lane showroom in London, and that Lexus could repurchase a car within two years.

## The LFA Nürburgring Package

Lexus announced the Nürburgring Package in 2011 as a factory option. Toyota's release lists a carbon-fibre front spoiler, a fixed rear wing, suspension set up for circuit use, special wheels and an output of 420 kW (571 PS), against 560 PS for the standard car. Lexus UK's figures are 562bhp against 552bhp, with the 0-62mph time unchanged at 3.7 seconds. PistonHeads adds that the suspension sits 10 mm lower, that the cabin gets carbon seats trimmed in Alcantara, and that the UK premium was £63,600. The Truth About Cars gives the US premium as $70,000.

On 31 August 2011, test driver Akira Iida lapped the 20.6 km Nordschleife in 7:14.64 in a Nürburgring Package car on Bridgestone Potenza RE070 tyres. Lexus called it a new fastest lap for a current, regular production car.

Lexus announced the package for 50 of the 500 cars, and The Truth About Cars was still told in 2012 that only 50 were being sold. Later auction listings, including the Bring a Trailer sale reported by Hypebeast in 2022, describe a total of 64 Nürburgring Package cars. Both figures appear in reputable places, so check the specific car's documentation.

## How the LFA was received

At launch the LFA was criticised for its price, which was about $375,000 in America and, according to PistonHeads, not far short of £340,000 in the UK. PistonHeads also notes that it was about 200 kg lighter than a Ferrari 599 with the same 0-62mph time. Its most famous admirer was Jeremy Clarkson. Writing in Top Gear magazine in early 2013, as reported by Lexus Enthusiast, he said he had "recently described it as the best car I've ever driven", and added that arguing the LFA was too expensive was "like arguing that, at £100 billion, the Mona Lisa is too expensive."

## LFA at the Nürburgring 24 Hours

The LFA's development was tied to the Nürburgring Nordschleife, and Toyota also took the car racing there. In 2009 Akio Toyoda, later Toyota's president, drove an LFA in the Nürburgring 24 Hours under an assumed name, a story told in our episode. Chief engineer Haruhiko Tanahashi told The Truth About Cars that the Nürburgring Package, though expensive, "shaved 8 seconds off our Nürburgring lap time."

## What to check when buying a Lexus LFA

The good news is that faults are rare. PistonHeads' 2021 guide says searching for known problems "won't reveal much" and that it had "never come across a car with such a stain-free record". The checks are mostly about history, use and the cost of parts.

**Clutch and gearbox.** The single-plate clutch can be damaged by aggressive launches, PistonHeads warns, because the car has no launch control. Shifts can feel jerky, especially in the fastest Sport setting, which is a trait of the gearbox rather than a fault.

**Service history.** Lexus told PistonHeads that LFA servicing is "more like servicing a race car", with more complexity and harder access than a normal road car. PistonHeads reports that Lexus UK's press car went to Toyota Motorsport's LFA Centre of Excellence in Cologne for a four-day check that included X-ray inspection of the brakes. A car's service history can be checked against its VIN through Lexus.

**Body and corrosion.** Some aluminium remains in the structure as crash-absorbing sections. PistonHeads had no reports of corrosion but suggests checking those areas on cars used in all weathers. Scuffs on the lower bodywork are common.

**Parts prices.** Low mileage does not mean low running costs. PistonHeads quotes about £2,750 per corner for carbon-ceramic discs, about £7,000 for a set of dampers, more than £14,000 for each bumper, about £6,500 for a windscreen and about £4,000 for the raised "F" badge. Many LFAs have covered very few miles, so tyres and fluids may be old even when the mileage is not.

**Warranty.** The original UK cover was four years or 50,000 miles, with six years or 70,000 miles on the drivetrain and six years against corrosion, so original warranties have long expired.

## How much is a Lexus LFA worth today?

The LFA has risen far beyond its list price. In July 2021 PistonHeads put dealer cars at from about £500,000, and Nürburgring Package cars nearer £700,000, noting that a 500-mile 2012 car, number 430, had sold for $720,000 at RM Sotheby's Amelia Island. In March 2022, Hypebeast reported that an orange Nürburgring Package car, number 349 with about 2,000 miles, sold on Bring a Trailer for $1,625,000.

The Classic Valuer, which tracks auction results, lists a median price of £605,280 across 41 LFA sales and a highest result of £1,368,577, about $1.81 million, achieved on Bring a Trailer on 29 April 2026. Its records show an 80 per cent sell-through rate across 51 auction appearances. Expect standard cars and Nürburgring Package cars to be priced as different markets, with colour, mileage and documentation making large differences within each.
