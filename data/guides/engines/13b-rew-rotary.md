## What is the 13B-REW?

The 13B-REW is the twin-turbocharged rotary engine of the third-generation Mazda RX-7, known by its chassis code FD3S. It is the last and most powerful turbocharged version of the 13B, Mazda's two-rotor Wankel engine family. Instead of pistons and valves, each rotor is a rounded triangle that turns on an eccentric shaft inside an oval-like housing, so each of its three faces takes in, compresses, burns and exhausts mixture in turn.

Each of the 13B's two chambers displaces 654 cc, giving 1,308 cc in total. Rotary displacement is not directly comparable with a piston engine's; evo describes the engine as equivalent to a 2.6-litre piston unit. DSport and HP Academy both give the 13B-REW's compression ratio as 9.0:1.

Output depended on market and year. Wikipedia lists 255 PS for Japanese cars from launch to 1995, 265 PS for 1996 to 1998 and 280 PS (about 276 hp) for the final cars to 2002, the last matching the Japanese gentlemen's agreement limit. evo gives 237 bhp at 6,500 rpm for UK cars, with a 7,000 rpm redline. Torque figures vary between sources: DSport gives 217 lb-ft, evo 218 lb-ft at 5,000 rpm, and HP Academy a range of 217 to 231 lb-ft across versions.

## 13B rotary history: from NSU licence to the FD RX-7

Mazda signed a technical agreement with NSU and Felix Wankel in July 1961 and launched its first rotary car, the Cosmo Sport, in May 1967. HP Academy traces the engine's growth from the 10A of about 1,000 cc, through the 1,200 cc 12A, to the 1,300 cc 13B in the early 1970s. HP Academy dates the 13B to 1973 and the RX-4; Wikipedia says Japanese use began in 1972. The 13B is essentially a longer 12A, with 80 mm-wide rotors rather than 70 mm.

Over the next three decades the 13B appeared in carburetted, fuel-injected, turbocharged and naturally aspirated forms. HP Academy lists the main versions as the 13B-AP of the 1970s, the fuel-injected 13B-RESI and 13B-DEI, the 13B-T of the second-generation (FC) RX-7 Turbo, the 13B-RE of the Japanese Eunos Cosmo, the 13B-REW of the FD RX-7 and the naturally aspirated 13B-MSP Renesis of the RX-8. The 13B-T, at up to 200 hp, was the first 13B to pass that mark.

Mazda launched the third-generation RX-7 in December 1991, and the 13B-REW stayed in production until the car ended in 2002, with the Spirit R limited edition of April 2002 as the final model. evo reports that Mazda built fewer than 70,000 FD RX-7s and stopped UK sales in 1995.

## How the sequential twin-turbo system works

The REW suffix refers to the sequential turbocharging. One turbocharger handles all the boost at lower engine speeds, which improves response from a small-capacity engine. Wikipedia says the primary works alone up to 4,500 rpm, after which the secondary comes on stream; evo describes a valve opening at about 4,500 rpm to let the second turbocharger contribute and controlled through a system of vacuum lines.

Mazda had used sequential twin turbochargers before the RX-7, on the Eunos Cosmo launched in April 1990, which was offered with the 13B-RE and the three-rotor 20B-REW. evo notes that the system needed precise electronic control of boost to work smoothly, and HP Academy describes the FD's sequential system as prone to failure.

## Design details that matter

Mazda's production rotors share a 105 mm rotor radius and a 15 mm eccentricity, the offset of the rotor journal from the shaft centre. On the 13B-REW, the intake ports are in the iron side housings and the exhaust ports are in the rotor housings. DSport lists steel rotors, aluminium rotor housings and iron side housings, primary and secondary fuel injectors and distributor ignition.

Because there are no valves or camshafts, a rotary has no valvetrain limit on engine speed. DSport notes that tuners gain the equivalent of camshaft and cylinder head upgrades by porting, reshaping the intake and exhaust ports in the housings. The compact, low engine also sits behind the front axle line in the FD, which DSport says lowers the car's centre of gravity.

## 13B-REW problems: why do apex seals fail?

Apex seals are the strips at each tip of the rotor that seal one chamber from the next, doing the job piston rings do in a conventional engine. Jalopnik reports that Mazda rotaries from roughly 1985 to 2002 used 2 mm three-piece cast-iron seals, which it describes as relatively durable in standard form. Their failures usually trace to detonation, wear, high-rpm chatter and, above all, insufficient lubrication. The rotary deliberately injects small amounts of oil into the combustion chambers to lubricate the seals, so it consumes oil by design and running low on oil is especially damaging.

HP Academy adds that factory seals are brittle and can shatter, particularly when carbon builds up in engines that are rarely driven hard, and that the 13B is very sensitive to detonation, misfires and lean running. A single detonation event can be enough to destroy an engine. Running hot for even a short period can also cause serious damage, and DSport lists heat management as a key concern on high-power engines.

Jalopnik advises oil changes every 2,500 to 3,000 miles or every three months, and reports that turbocharged rotaries maintained this way have been reported to last 60,000 miles or more before needing a rebuild. Symptoms of worn seals include hard cold starts, a rough idle, blue smoke, misfires and lost power. evo's view is that most FDs will need an engine rebuild at some point, because Mazda never fully solved apex seal wear.

Other weaknesses listed by DSport include short spark plug life, poor fuel economy, high fuel demands and modest torque.

## How much power can a 13B-REW make?

HP Academy's figures give a sense of the range, though they come from tuners rather than controlled tests. It puts a turbocharged 13B at around 400 hp at the wheels with supporting upgrades, and reports 420 hp at the wheels from its own FD project on standard ports. With stud and dowel kits, street porting and a large single turbocharger, it reports about 700 hp at the wheels, and says race engines on methanol have gone well beyond that.

HP Academy also stresses that the factory ECU in the RX-7 cannot be reflashed, so tuning requires aftermarket engine management, and that most reliability problems in modified rotaries come from poor tuning, building and maintenance rather than the design itself.

## The 13B's legacy and the rotary today

The four-rotor R26B, displacing 2,616 cc, powered the Mazda 787B to victory at the 1991 Le Mans 24 Hours. The 13B continued in naturally aspirated Renesis form in the RX-8 until production of that car ended in June 2012. Mazda resumed rotary production in June 2023, using a rotary engine as a range-extending generator in the MX-30 e-Skyactiv R-EV, and announced in October 2023 that it had built more than two million rotary-engined vehicles.
