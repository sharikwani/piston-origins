## What is the RB26DETT?

The RB26DETT is Nissan's 2.6-litre, twin-turbocharged inline six-cylinder engine, built for the Skyline GT-R. It powered the R32, R33 and R34 generations of the car between 1989 and 2002. The name breaks down as RB for the engine family, 26 for 2.6 litres, D for double overhead camshafts, E for electronic fuel injection and TT for twin turbochargers.

It displaces 2,568 cc from a bore of 86 mm and a stroke of 73.7 mm, with a compression ratio of 8.5:1. The block is cast iron and the cylinder head aluminium, with four valves per cylinder. Nissan's own heritage records give the R32 GT-R's output as 206 kW (280 PS) at 6,800 rpm and 353 Nm (36 kg·m) at 4,400 rpm. That 280 PS (about 276 hp) figure was the ceiling of the Japanese manufacturers' gentlemen's agreement, and it stayed the official rating for every generation. Wikipedia notes that enthusiasts generally believe the true output was nearer 320 PS.

## Why 2.6 litres? The RB26's Group A origins

The RB26DETT was developed for racing first. Japanese magazine Best Car says the engine was created to win Group A touring-car racing in the All Japan Touring Car Championship, and that its 2,568 cc displacement was chosen to fit the Group A rules. Under those rules, turbocharged engines had their capacity multiplied by 1.7 to set their class. Wikipedia reports that a 2.4-litre engine was first considered for the 4,000 cc class, but once four-wheel drive made the car heavier, the project moved to 2.6 litres and the 4,500 cc class (2,568 cc multiplied by 1.7 is about 4,366 cc).

The plan worked. Nissan records 29 wins and no defeats for the R32 GT-R across four Japan Touring Car Championship seasons from 1990 to 1993. Best Car says the racing engines produced more than 600 PS.

## RB26DETT design details

Several features set the RB26DETT apart from Nissan's other RB engines. Instead of a single throttle, it uses six individual throttle bodies, arranged as three siamesed pairs. The valvetrain uses solid lifters with shims under the buckets, a layout DSport magazine credits with allowing very high engine speeds, and the camshafts are belt driven. The short 73.7 mm stroke also favours revs.

Wikipedia lists cast pistons with cooling channels, oil squirters beneath the pistons, sodium-filled exhaust valves, I-beam connecting rods and an eight-counterweight crankshaft. DSport lists 440 cc injectors, coil-on-plug ignition and dual valve springs. The two turbochargers work in parallel rather than in sequence, and both Wikipedia and Best Car note that they use ceramic turbine wheels.

## RB26DETT variants and generation changes

Nissan revised the engine during its production life. Wikipedia records a later torque rating of 392 Nm at 4,400 rpm by the end of production, up from the R32's 353 Nm; sources disagree on the exact figure for the R33, so it is not given here. The R34 GT-R received ball-bearing turbochargers with ceramic exhaust wheels, ignition modules built into the coil packs, a different crank angle sensor drive and a dual-mass flywheel.

For racing and homologation, Nismo produced the N1 version, which Wikipedia says came about because the standard engine needed too much maintenance in competition. It has a more precisely balanced crankshaft, improved water and oil passages, upgraded camshafts and turbochargers with steel turbine wheels. The N1 block carries the casting mark 24U, against 05U for the standard block. DSport also describes a more heavily reinforced block supplied to race teams.

At the top of the range, the Nismo Z-tune R34 used a 2.8-litre version of the engine with a stroked crankshaft and larger turbochargers, rated at 500 PS and 540 Nm. There was also a naturally aspirated RB26DE, rated at 220 PS, in the 1992 Autech Version GTS-4 saloon.

## Which cars had the RB26DETT?

Production cars with the engine were the BNR32 Skyline GT-R, the BCNR33 Skyline GT-R, the BNR34 Skyline GT-R and the WGNC34 Stagea 260RS estate. Wikipedia also lists the four-door Autech 40th Anniversary R33 and the Tommykaira ZZII concept.

## How much power can an RB26 make?

Tuning figures for the RB26 come mainly from tuners and magazines rather than controlled tests. DSport says the factory turbochargers run out at about 400 hp at the wheels, that the standard block is good for more than 800 hp at the wheels, and that fully built engines have reached more than 1,400 hp. The aftermarket offers stroker kits, deck plates and overbore options that take displacement beyond 3.0 litres.

Those figures depend on supporting work. DSport lists the cost of upgrades as one of the engine's drawbacks, alongside its modest displacement and lack of factory variable valve timing.

## RB26DETT problems and known weaknesses

The best-known weakness is oil pump drive on early engines. Wikipedia explains that on pre-1992 R32 engines the part of the crankshaft that drives the oil pump was machined too narrow, which could break the pump at high engine speeds. Later R32 engines and all R33 and R34 engines used a wider drive, and aftermarket collars and spline-drive conversions exist for early cranks. DSport describes the standard oil pump as prone to failure, rates the factory N1 pump as an improvement and aftermarket pumps as better still.
Wikipedia also notes that the standard crankshaft vibrates between 7,000 and 8,000 rpm, one reason Nismo balanced the N1 crank to a higher standard. The ceramic turbine wheels in the factory turbochargers are another limit once boost is raised.

## RB26 vs 2JZ: how do they compare?

The comparison comes up because both are iron-block, twin-turbo, 24-valve inline-sixes from the same era, and HP Academy says Toyota developed the 2JZ-GTE partly to answer the RB26. On paper they differ in approach. The RB26 is smaller, at 2,568 cc against 2,997 cc, with a much shorter stroke (73.7 mm against 86 mm), individual throttle bodies and a design shaped by racing rules. The 2JZ-GTE has more displacement and therefore more torque, sequential rather than parallel turbochargers, and a closed-deck block.

Both were officially rated at 280 PS in Japan. In the US, where the RB26 was never sold new, the 2JZ-GTE Supra was rated at 320 hp and 315 lb-ft. On the tuning side, Jalopnik reports that most tuners consider the 2JZ-GTE's standard bottom end good for about 800 hp, while DSport puts the standard RB26 block at more than 800 hp at the wheels; both figures are tuner estimates rather than factory limits. The clearest difference is the one DSport itself lists as an RB26 drawback: its smaller displacement gives it less torque than larger engines such as the 2JZ. The RB26's case rests on its short-stroke, high-revving design and its unbeaten Group A record in Japan.
