## What is the 1LR-GUE?

The 1LR-GUE is the 4.8-litre, naturally aspirated V10 that powers the Lexus LFA, the carbon-fibre supercar Toyota built in a run of 500 cars between December 2010 and December 2012. It was co-developed and produced by Yamaha under commission from Toyota, and it was used in no other production car.

Lexus's official specification gives a displacement of 4,805 cc, maximum output of 412 kW (560 PS) at 8,700 rpm and peak torque of 480 Nm at 6,800 rpm, with a 9,000 rpm redline. In the United States the output is usually quoted as 552 hp and 354 lb-ft; Jalopnik's launch report gave 553 hp. The cylinder banks sit at 72 degrees, and PistonHeads' published technical specification lists the bore and stroke as 88 mm by 79 mm and the compression ratio as 12.0:1.

Sources differ slightly on the torque peak. Lexus's launch release gives 6,800 rpm, while Yamaha's own page for the engine lists 7,000 rpm. The power and torque figures themselves agree.

## Why did Lexus build a V10? The LFA engine's development

The LFA took the best part of a decade to develop; evo says Lexus spent nine years on it. The engine's brief followed from the car's construction. As chief engineer Haruhiko Tanahashi told The Truth About Cars, "It makes no sense to save all that weight with composites, only to waste it on a heavy engine."

Toyota's description, as reported in the same series, is that the V10 is as small as a traditional V8, as light as a conventional V6 and delivers the power of a V12. Lexus's launch release says only that the engine is smaller than a conventional V8. Toyota engineers declined to give The Truth About Cars an engine weight, so this guide does not quote one.

The engine is mounted behind the front axle line, with the gearbox at the rear as a transaxle connected by a torque tube. The Truth About Cars reports a 48:52 front-to-rear weight distribution.

## What Yamaha did on the LFA engine

Yamaha's role went beyond supplying parts. Its corporate collection page says Yamaha co-developed and produced the 1LR-GUE for Toyota and describes it as one of the most high-end projects in its automotive engine work. To meet the quality targets for such a small run, Yamaha adapted its cell production method and built one engine per day. Each engine carries an aluminium plate bearing the name of the craftsman who assembled it, and PistonHeads notes that a single builder was responsible for each unit and signed it off.

Yamaha also worked on the sound. Its page says the exhaust note was tuned together with Yamaha Corporation, the musical instrument company, and likens the result to "the roar of an angel". The finished engines were delivered pre-assembled to Toyota's Motomachi plant, where the cars were built.

## 1LR-GUE design details

Lexus's launch material lists titanium valves, lightweight rocker arms with a diamond-like coating and a fully integrated lower crankcase. Jalopnik adds titanium connecting rods, a titanium exhaust manifold and forged aluminium pistons. PistonHeads describes the engine as built from aluminium, titanium alloy and magnesium. The engine uses dry-sump lubrication, and Lexus placed the oil and water pumps behind the engine.

Each of the ten cylinders has its own electronically controlled throttle body, which Lexus says roughly halves response time compared with a single throttle. The intake side uses an acoustically tuned surge tank, and the exhaust uses equal-length manifolds feeding an equal-length dual system with a multistage titanium silencer. Lexus says 90 per cent of peak torque is available between 3,700 rpm and 9,000 rpm.

The figure most people know is the rev speed. Lexus claimed the unloaded engine could rise from idle to its 9,000 rpm redline in 0.6 seconds, a figure reported by both evo and PistonHeads. Evo's account of the instrument design says that responsiveness shaped the LFA's rev counter, a TFT screen showing a virtual dial in place of a mechanical instrument, developed by Tanahashi's team with help from Gran Turismo developer Polyphony Digital. PistonHeads adds that fuel is cut at 9,500 rpm and that the gearbox will upshift itself at 9,200 rpm.

## LFA engine variants: the Nürburgring Package

There was only one version of the 1LR-GUE, but the Nürburgring Package raised its output. PistonHeads' technical specification lists 419 kW (570 PS) at 8,900 rpm for the package, against 560 PS for the standard car. The Truth About Cars reports that 50 Nürburgring Package cars were sold, at a $70,000 premium, and that the changes included slight alterations in the engine bay. On 31 August 2011 an LFA Nürburgring Package lapped the Nordschleife in 7:14.64.

## Cars fitted with the 1LR-GUE

The 1LR-GUE was fitted only to the Lexus LFA. All 500 cars, including the Nürburgring Package examples, used it with a six-speed automated sequential gearbox. Lexus quoted a price of approximately $375,000 when the car went on sale.

## 1LR-GUE reliability and servicing

Because the engine was never sold separately and the cars cover few miles, there is little tuning culture around the 1LR-GUE. PistonHeads' used-buying guide describes reliability as "fantastic" and says searching for known faults "won't reveal much". Servicing is a different matter: Lexus told PistonHeads it was "more like servicing a race car", with more complexity and access issues than a road car. Original warranty cover in the UK was four years or 50,000 miles, extended to six years or 70,000 miles for the drivetrain.

## What is a Lexus LFA worth today?

The LFA cost about $375,000 new, and values have risen well past that. PistonHeads' guide put dealer cars at close to £500,000, and nearer £700,000 for a Nürburgring Package car, at the time of writing. Classic Valuer gives a median price of £605,280 and records the highest LFA sale as £1,368,577, achieved on Bring a Trailer on 29 April 2026.
