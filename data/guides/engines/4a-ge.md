## What is the 4A-GE?

The 4A-GE is Toyota's 1.6-litre, twin-cam four-cylinder engine, best known as the engine of the AE86 Corolla Levin and Sprinter Trueno. It displaces 1,587 cc from a bore of 81 mm and a stroke of 77 mm, uses a cast-iron block and an aluminium cylinder head, and was built in five main generations between 1983 and 2000. Jalopnik puts its factory output at between 112 and 160 hp depending on version.

In the AE86, the first-generation engine was rated in Japan at 130 PS on the gross measurement used there at the time. Export ratings were lower and varied by market: Wikipedia lists 124 PS for most of Europe and 114 PS (112 hp) for North America, where the car was sold as the Corolla GT-S. evo quotes 123 bhp at 6,600 rpm and 107 lb-ft at 5,200 rpm, with a 7,000 rpm limit, in line with the European figure.

## 4A-GE history and the Yamaha connection

The 4A-GE went on sale in May 1983, the launch date Toyota gives for the AE86. It was a twin-cam, 16-valve development of Toyota's 4A family, and both evo and DSport magazine report that its cylinder head was developed with Yamaha. evo points out that the same partnership later produced the Lexus LFA's V10.

Jalopnik notes that the engine was designed to work in both front- and rear-wheel-drive cars and to be mounted either transversely or longitudinally. That flexibility explains why the same engine went into the rear-drive AE86, the front-drive Corolla FX16 and the mid-engined MR2.

## 4A-GE generations: blue top, red top, silver top and black top

Enthusiasts identify the generations by the colour of the cam cover lettering. Wikipedia gives the following outline.

The first-generation **"blue top"** (1983 to 1987) is known as the big-port engine for its large intake ports. It uses a 9.4:1 compression ratio (10.0:1 in Europe) and T-VIS, a set of butterfly valves that closes off half the intake runners below about 4,200 rpm to improve low-speed response. Japanese engines measured airflow with a MAP sensor, while US and European engines used an airflow meter.

The second-generation **"red and black top"** (1987 to 1989) kept T-VIS and the big ports but added larger 42 mm connecting-rod big ends and extra ribbing on the block.

The third-generation **"red top"** (1989 to 1991) is the small-port engine. It dropped T-VIS, received smaller intake ports, oil squirters for piston cooling and thicker connecting rods, and rose to 10.3:1 compression and 140 PS in Japan.

The fourth-generation **"silver top"** (1991 to 1995) introduced a 20-valve head with five valves per cylinder, variable valve timing on the intake camshaft and individual throttle bodies. It was rated at 160 PS at 7,400 rpm. The fifth-generation **"black top"** (1995 to 2000) raised compression to 11.0:1, replaced the airflow meter with a MAP sensor, used larger 45 mm throttle bodies and made 165 PS at 7,800 rpm.

Alongside these, Toyota built the supercharged 4A-GZE, which uses a Roots-type supercharger and lower compression. Wikipedia lists outputs from 145 PS in the first version to 170 PS in the last.

## Design details that matter

DSport lists the 4A-GE's valvetrain as shim-over-bucket, its camshafts at 240 degrees of duration for big-port engines and 232 degrees for small-port engines, and describes the cylinder head as non-interference, meaning valves and pistons should not collide if the timing belt fails. DSport calls the cast-iron block a durable foundation, though it also counts the engine's weight among its drawbacks.

One detail matters to AE86 owners in particular. DSport points out that no rear-wheel-drive car received the 20-valve head with variable valve timing from the factory; it was first used in the front-drive AE101.

## Which cars had the 4A-GE?

According to Wikipedia, the 16-valve engine was fitted to the AE86 Corolla Levin and Sprinter Trueno, the AE82 Corolla and Sprinter, the AA63 Celica and Carina, the AE92 Corolla and Sprinter, the AT160 Celica, Corona and Carina in Japan, the AW11 MR2 and the US-market Geo Prizm GSi. The 20-valve engines went into the AE101 and AE111 Corolla Levin and Sprinter Trueno coupés and related Japanese Corolla and Sprinter models. The supercharged 4A-GZE was used in the AW11 MR2 and the Japanese AE92 and AE101 Corolla and Sprinter.

## How much horsepower can a 4A-GE make?

A standard 4A-GE is not powerful, and DSport is blunt about it. It calls the engine overhyped, describing standard output as uninspiring and attributing much of its popularity to the AE86, drifting and the Initial D comic and anime. It also lists a heavy iron block, small displacement, a poorly designed factory intake manifold and a tendency for head gaskets to fail.

DSport's advice for more power is that camshaft and cylinder head work is essential, with individual throttle bodies or carburettors replacing the factory intake. Forced induction and nitrous are other routes, and DSport suggests that a more modern four-cylinder swap may make more sense for anyone chasing outright power. At the top end, Wikipedia reports that the 4A-GE in full Formula Atlantic racing trim produced 250 PS at 10,000 rpm, which shows what a fully developed race engine can do but is not a realistic figure for a road car.

## 4A-GE reputation and parts supply today

The 4A-GE's appeal lies more in how it delivers power than in how much. In the AE86 it revs to 7,000 rpm, and evo notes that the Corollas it went into rarely weighed more than 1,000 kg.

Parts supply has improved. In September 2025, Toyota Gazoo Racing announced that its GR Heritage Parts project would reproduce the 4A-GE cylinder head and cylinder block as sub-assemblies. Toyota says the new head has extra machining in the combustion chambers to reduce variation in compression ratio, coated intake ports, a full set of cam cap knock pins and thicker walls on some ports. The block is honed with a modern process, uses a higher-rigidity cast iron and has a revised crank-cap structure, while keeping mounting points for both rear-drive and front-drive cars. Toyota scheduled sales for around May 2026, with the caveat that low orders could delay the launch. The head is sold without camshafts and valves, and the block without crankshaft and pistons.
