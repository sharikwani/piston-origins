Toyota AE86 Buyer's Guide: Specs, Trims, Rust and Values

Updated 2026-11-09 · 8 sources

What is the Toyota AE86?

The AE86 is the rear-wheel-drive, twin-cam version of the fifth-generation Toyota Corolla coupé, sold in Japan as the Corolla Levin and the Sprinter Trueno from 1983 to 1987. The Levin has fixed headlamps and the Trueno has pop-ups; underneath they are the same car. Toyota's own description is that only cars fitted with the 1.6-litre DOHC 16-valve engine carry the AE86 code, and that the nickname hachi-roku is Japanese for "eight-six". Our episode tells how a cheap Corolla became a drift legend; this page is for anyone looking to buy one.

Toyota AE86 specs

The AE86 uses the 4A-GE, a 1,587 cc twin-cam four with a bore and stroke of 81 by 77 mm, covered in detail in our engine guide. In Japan it was rated at 130 PS at 6,600 rpm and 149 Nm at 5,200 rpm on the gross measurement used there at the time. Export ratings were lower: JDMBUYSELL lists 124 PS DIN for the European Corolla GT, and the US Corolla GT-S was rated at 112 hp.

Weights are low by modern standards. JDMBUYSELL, quoting Japanese catalogue data, gives 940 kg for a Levin GTV three-door and 960 kg for a Levin GT-APEX three-door, while Goodwood quotes 970 kg. Goodwood lists 0-62mph in 8.3 seconds and a top speed of 122mph for a 130 PS car. The US car was slower: Car and Driver timed the Corolla GT-S at 9.5 seconds to 60 mph. A five-speed manual was standard, with a four-speed automatic offered on some grades from May 1985.

AE86 trims: GT, GTV, GT-APEX and the US GT-S

Two body styles were offered: a two-door notchback coupé and a three-door liftback, which owners usually call the hatch. Goodwood and JDMBUYSELL agree on the Japanese grades. The GT was the coupé only and had vented front discs. The GTV was a liftback only, manual only, with rear discs, stiffer suspension and fewer comforts, which makes it the enthusiast's pick. The GT-APEX was the best-equipped grade, available in both body styles, with rear discs and an optional limited-slip differential.

The best-known special edition is the Sprinter Trueno GT-APEX Black Limited of 1986, a three-door with gold emblems and decals and orange-stitched trim. JDMBUYSELL gives the run as 400 cars and says it was mechanically the same as a GT-APEX, so a genuine one needs paperwork as well as decals.

Production is usually split into the early zenki cars (1983 to 1986) and the facelifted kouki cars (1986 to 1987). Export versions differed. The UK and Ireland received the Corolla GT, a Levin-based liftback; Goodwood calls the UK-market car rarer than a Japanese import. America's twin-cam car was the Corolla GT-S, which cost $11,530 new in 1985, while the cheaper US SR5 used a carburetted single-cam engine and is not an AE86.

How many AE86s were made?

Toyota has not published a clear production breakdown for the AE86 in the sources we could check, so we do not quote a total. What is known is the run: the Levin and Trueno went on sale in Japan on 12 May 1983 and were replaced by the front-wheel-drive AE92 in May 1987. Limited editions such as the Black Limited are documented separately.

How the AE86 was received

It was never a fast car on paper, and period figures like Car and Driver's 9.5-second 0-60 time say so plainly. In America it was priced and sold as an economy car, and most buyers walked past it. Its reputation rests on light weight, rear-wheel drive and an engine that loves to rev, and Goodwood describes the manual car as the one that gives the full experience. It found most of its following later, through motorsport and through the people who drove it hard.

AE86 in motorsport, drifting and Initial D

Keiichi Tsuchiya, the "Drift King", raced an AE86 in the All-Japan Touring Car Championship and helped turn drifting from a mountain-road habit into a sport. In 1995 Shuichi Shigeno's manga Initial D made a white and black Sprinter Trueno its hero car, and the price premium that followed is known among fans as the Takumi tax. Toyota itself acknowledges the lineage: its newsroom says the 86 of 2012 and the GR86 inherited the AE86's culture.

AE86 common problems and what to look for

Rust is the first concern. Goodwood's January 2026 buyer's guide lists the sunroof surrounds, sills, suspension mounts and spare wheel well as rust areas, and names rust and crash damage as the two main worries. Owners on the AEU86 forum add the area under the windscreen, the pockets at the sides of the boot on two-door cars, the door sills and the footwells under the carpets, where water collects, and warn that a car can look fine from above while being rotten underneath. Engines can be replaced far more easily than a clean, straight shell, so the body deserves most of the inspection time.

Make sure it is a real AE86. The single-cam AE85 shares the Levin and Trueno names and was sold alongside it. Goodwood warns that AE85s are sometimes passed off as twin-cam AE86s, especially through importers. JDMBUYSELL advises checking the chassis code on the shell and the registration papers, because an engine swap does not turn an AE85 into an AE86. In the US, the same applies to SR5s dressed up as GT-Ss.

Hard lives and modifications. Goodwood notes that many cars have been drifted, rallied or modified, and that unmodified, rust-free cars are hard to find. Sympathetic changes can add appeal, but DIY work needs checking. Missing original trim is another problem, because correct replacement parts are scarce.

Mechanicals. The 4A-GE is generally robust, but oil leaks from the cam cover and seals are common, and JDMBUYSELL advises treating the cambelt as due unless there is a dated record of its replacement. Listen for worn synchromesh and check the clutch. Goodwood points out that the unassisted steering is heavy at low speed and that a limited-slip differential is a desirable extra.

Parts. Toyota Gazoo Racing began reproducing AE86 parts under its GR Heritage Parts programme on 1 November 2021, starting with rear brake calipers and steering knuckle arms, followed by rear driveshafts from 1 December 2021. Toyota says the parts are made for a limited period and sold while stocks last, and availability varies by region. New 4A-GE heads and blocks were announced in 2025; see the engine guide for details.

How much is a Toyota AE86 worth?

Prices rose sharply around 2021 and have since spread out according to condition and originality. In May 2021 a 112,000-mile 1985 Corolla GT-S, the US-market AE86, sold on Bring a Trailer for $40,000. In July 2021 Motoring Research reported that a one-owner, 92,000-mile 1987 UK Corolla GT in original Wine Red sold through Car & Classic for £46,250, a record at the time; it had cost £12,500 new.

More recent results are lower for ordinary cars. A 146,000-mile 1985 GT-S with replaced panels and underside rust sold on Bring a Trailer on 3 January 2025 for $23,000. At Iconic Auctioneers on 8 November 2025, a restored 1986 UK Corolla Twin Cam estimated at £35,000 to £45,000 did not sell. The Classic Valuer's tracker, based on a small number of auction results since 2020, lists a median AE86 price of £29,470 and a highest sale of £50,625.
