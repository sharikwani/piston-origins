Mazda RX-7 FD (FD3S) Buyer's Guide: Specs, Problems, Values

Updated 2026-11-09 · 10 sources

Mazda RX-7 FD at a glance

The third-generation Mazda RX-7, chassis code FD3S, went on sale in Japan in December 1991 and was built until August 2002. It was the last RX-7 and the last car to use the 13B-REW twin-turbo rotary. The episode page explains how Mazda made the rotary work and how the FD fits into that story; this page is for owners and buyers who want the specs, the variants, the weak points and current prices in one place.

The car was designed with input from Mazda's California studio, with a team that included Tom Matano, and is a compact front-engined, rear-wheel-drive coupé sold as a two-seater or a 2+2 depending on market and trim. It was sold in the US for the 1993 to 1995 model years and in the UK until 1995 or 1996, depending on the source, but stayed on sale in Japan for a decade.

Mazda RX-7 FD specs

Every FD used the 13B-REW, a twin-rotor engine of 2 × 654 cc with sequential twin turbochargers; the second turbo joins in at about 4,500 rpm. Power rose through the car's life. JDMBuySell's dated grade table gives 255 PS at launch in December 1991, a 265 PS headline rating from January 1996 and 280 PS for the Type R and Type RS from January 1999, the last matching the Japanese gentlemen's agreement ceiling of 280 PS (about 276 hp). US cars were rated at 255 hp, and evo lists the UK car at 237 bhp at 6,500 rpm with 218 lb ft at 5,000 rpm.

The standard gearbox was a five-speed manual, with a four-speed automatic on some trims, and a Torsen limited-slip differential and forged aluminium double wishbones were part of the specification. evo gives a weight of 1,310 kg for the UK car, a claimed 0-60 mph time of 5.3 seconds and a limited top speed of 156 mph. Mazda's Spirit R press release notes that the Type A's lightweight Recaro seats alone saved about 10 kg.

RX-7 FD trims and special editions

Japan had a long and confusing list of grades, many sold under Mazda's Efini brand. JDMBuySell's catalogue-based table lists the launch Type S, X and R, then the two-seat Type R-II and the Touring S and X automatics from August 1993. From January 1999, the Series 8 cars were sold as the Type RS, Type R and Type RB, with the RB rated at 265 PS as a manual and 255 PS as an automatic.

Limited editions followed. The Type RS-R of October 1997 marked 30 years of the rotary and was limited to 500 cars, a two-seat Type RZ of October 2000 was limited to 175, and the Type R Bathurst R of August 2001 was limited to 500 for Japan. The Bathurst name recognises the FD's three wins in Australia's Bathurst 12 Hour production-car race, according to Repco's history of the model.

The final car was the Spirit R. Mazda's own press release of March 2002 announced 1,500 cars, sold only in Japan from 22 April 2002, in three versions: the two-seat Type A and four-seat Type B, both five-speed manuals with 280 PS and drilled brakes, and the Type C automatic with 255 PS. All three had red brake calipers, 17-inch BBS wheels and a Nardi steering wheel, and Titanium Grey Metallic paint was exclusive to them.

In the US the range was the base car, the Touring with leather and a sunroof, and the R1, renamed R2 for 1994, which added firmer suspension, an extra oil cooler and aero parts. The Autopian notes that the R1 and R2 are essentially the same package.

How many FD RX-7s were made?

Mazda UK's figure, quoted by JDMBuySell, is 68,589 FD RX-7s for all markets, and evo says fewer than 70,000 were sold in ten years. Around 14,000 were sold in the US, as the episode page notes. UK numbers are small but disputed: PistonHeads says sales are estimated in the hundreds, with 164 reportedly sold through dealers, while evo says "only a few hundred"; other tallies run slightly higher. Most FDs in Britain today are Japanese imports.

How the RX-7 FD was received

Reviewers loved the chassis and the looks; buyers balked at the price. PistonHeads calls it a "tough sell" in the UK, where it launched at £34,000 by its account (evo says around £32,000) before being cut to about £25,000 to £26,000. evo's retrospective review calls it "the high point of Mazda's rotary efforts" and "a thoroughly charming, fascinating, intriguing and usefully rapid car", while listing high fuel consumption and dated interior plastics among its faults.

RX-7 FD in motorsport and pop culture

In Australia the FD won the Bathurst 12 Hour three times in the early 1990s, and Mazda Australia also developed the RX-7 SP to take on Porsche, BMW and Lotus in production racing. For most fans, though, the FD is the yellow car from the anime Initial D and Han's orange and black car in Tokyo Drift, both covered on the episode page.

RX-7 FD common problems: what to check

The engine comes first. PistonHeads' buying guide recommends a rotary compression test by a specialist and says readings between 7 and 8 bar are good, while lower figures suggest worn apex and side seals; it put a specialist rebuild at about £3,000 in 2017. Oil consumption of around a quarter of a litre per 1,000 miles is normal, and higher use is a warning sign. Steam from the exhaust on start-up can point to failing coolant seals. evo says that, apart from the most carefully maintained cars, most FDs will need an engine rebuild at some point. The 13B-REW engine guide explains why apex seals fail.

Heat is the FD's other enemy. PistonHeads warns that turbo control solenoids and rubber turbo pipes fail from under-bonnet heat, causing erratic changeover between the turbos, and that the coolant air-separator tank can split and leak. It recommends changing the coolant every year. If an engine floods on a cold start, remove and dry the spark plugs rather than continuing to crank it.

The five-speed gearbox is tough, but a poor shift from fourth to fifth is common. On the body, check the chassis rails and suspension turrets for creases that suggest an old impact, look for rust in the rear wheel arches, where water can enter through the rear wing mounts, and remember that replacement panels are hard to find. Special-edition headlight covers are close to impossible to replace.

How much is an FD RX-7 worth?

Unmodified, low-mileage US cars have become valuable. The Autopian reported that a 1994 R2 in Silverstone Metallic with 6,743 miles sold for $148,000 on Bring a Trailer in June 2023, and a 1993 car with 8,253 miles sold for $80,500 there in October 2023. The Classic Valuer records a 1993 R1 in Competition Yellow Mica with 20,000 miles selling on Bring a Trailer on 4 November 2026 for the equivalent of £87,690.

Ordinary imports are far cheaper. JDMBuySell's listing data from 9 November 2026 puts the median asking price at US$38,670 across 251 FDs, with the middle half between US$31,263 and US$45,520; most of those cars were listed in Japan. These are asking prices, not sales. In the UK, PistonHeads in October 2017 put an immaculate UK-supplied car from £20,000 and a good import at about half that, though prices have moved since.

Importing an RX-7 FD into the US or UK

In the US, the 25-year exemption runs from each car's own manufacture date, so every Japanese-market FD is now old enough or will be by late 2027, when the last 2002 builds qualify. JDMBuySell notes that the EPA generally expects the original engine configuration, which matters for an engine that is often rebuilt or swapped, and that state registration rules vary.

In the UK, importing a Japanese FD means an import declaration, VAT and duty, notifying HMRC within 14 days, vehicle approval where required and DVLA registration; GOV.UK sets out the steps. PistonHeads also warns that a car with its air pump removed may struggle with the emissions part of the MOT.
