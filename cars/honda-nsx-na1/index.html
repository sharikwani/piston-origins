Honda NSX NA1 Buyer's Guide: Specs, Type R, Problems, Values

Updated 2026-11-09 · 8 sources

What is the first-generation Honda NSX?

The first-generation NSX is Honda's mid-engined, two-seat sports car, built from 1990 to 2005 and sold in North America as the Acura NSX. Its all-aluminium body, a V6 with VTEC and titanium connecting rods, and Ayrton Senna's part in its development are covered in our episode and in the C30A engine guide. This page is about the car as you would buy it today: the specifications, the many variants, how many were made, what goes wrong and what they sell for.

The chassis code NA1 covers the 3.0-litre cars; NA2 is the code for the 3.2-litre cars from 1997. Honda Japan put the NA1 on sale on 14 September 1990, and the last cars were built in late 2005.

Honda NSX NA1 specs

The launch car used the 2,977 cc C30A V6, mounted transversely behind the cabin. evo lists the UK car at 270bhp at 7,300rpm and 210lb ft, with a 0-62mph time of 5.9 seconds and a top speed of 168mph. Car Throttle quotes the same 270 hp for the US car and a factory weight of 3,009 lb (1,365 kg). In Japan the manual engine was rated at 280 PS, the figure set by the domestic power agreement of the time. Cars with the four-speed automatic used a softer 252 hp tune.

From 1997 the manual NSX moved to the 3.2-litre C32B with a six-speed gearbox. Car Throttle gives 290 hp at 7,100 rpm and 221 lb-ft, along with larger front brakes and 17-inch wheels. Weight crept up as equipment and the targa roof arrived: Wikipedia's model-year table lists 1,365 kg for 1991–92 cars and about 1,435 kg for 1997–2001 cars. evo gives 1,370 kg for the coupé and 1,415 kg for the targa.

NSX trims, variants and special editions

The NSX-T arrived in March 1995 with a removable targa roof. evo notes that it brought around 45 kg of extra strengthening, electric power steering and revised spring and damper rates. Car Throttle says enthusiasts generally prefer the stiffer coupé.

The NSX Type R (NSX-R) went on sale in Japan on 27 November 1992. It was a stripped, five-speed-only coupé with Recaro seats, a MOMO wheel and stiffer suspension, and RM Sotheby's catalogue describes it as up to 120 kg lighter than a standard car. RM Sotheby's and other auction sources put production at 483, all for the Japanese market. JDMBUYSELL notes that one Honda timeline gives 500, so expect to see both figures.

For the NA2, Japan received the Type S (1,320 kg, with air conditioning retained) and the circuit-focused Type S-Zero (1,270 kg, no air conditioning or audio). JDMBUYSELL, citing Honda's figures, gives 209 Type S and 30 S-Zero cars. America got the Zanardi Edition in 1999, a lightweight coupé honouring Alex Zanardi's CART titles. Acura's launch material spoke of 50 numbered cars; the usual total is 51, because one extra car served as the press car.

The facelift arrived in Japan in December 2001, replacing the pop-up headlamps with fixed xenon units. A second NSX-R followed in May 2002, again for Japan only, at about 1,270 kg; Honda sources give roughly 140 to 150 cars. Five NSX-R GT homologation cars were built in 2005, and only one was sold.

How many Honda NSXs were made?

Honda built just over 18,000 first-generation NSXs, a figure Car Throttle and Wikipedia agree on. JDMBUYSELL cites a global sales total of 18,685, which includes cars sold after production ended. evo puts it as fewer than 20,000 in 16 years. Wikipedia's North American table works out at roughly 9,000 cars for the US and about 700 for Canada, so around half of all NSXs went to North America. The UK took only tens of cars a year, and evo counted roughly 250 left there in 2025, more than 150 of them declared off the road.

How the NSX was received

Contemporary reaction centred on the idea of a usable exotic. evo recalls a Performance Car cover line that read "Drives like a Ford, goes like a Ferrari", and Brian Laban's description of it as a "crypto-Ferrari". Performance Car named it its Car of the Year in 1991, and the 2002 NSX-R won evo's Car of the Year contest. evo also notes that Gordon Murray used an NSX as a benchmark while developing the McLaren F1.

It was not cheap. evo gives a UK price of £55,000 in 1991, a little above a Porsche 964 Carrera 2 and well below a Ferrari 348. Sales were modest: evo says the UK took tens of cars a year when Porsche was selling hundreds of 911s.

NSX in motorsport and Gran Turismo

The NSX raced at Le Mans in the mid-1990s. In 1995 the Team Kunimitsu car, number 84, driven by Keiichi Tsuchiya, Akira Iida and Kunimitsu Takahashi, finished eighth overall and won the GT2 class. In Japan the NSX-GT became a mainstay of the JGTC and later Super GT series. A generation of players first met the car in Gran Turismo, where the GT2 car appeared in the 1997 original.

Honda NSX common problems and what to check

The NSX has a strong reputation for reliability, but it is now an old, complex car. Car Throttle's buying guide and evo's used review agree on the main points.

Body and crash damage. The aluminium structure does not rust in the usual way, and evo says corrosion is not a problem. Crash damage is the real risk. Car Throttle warns that aluminium repairs are hard and costly, that many body shops will not touch them, and that a bent chassis may never be fully straight again. A magnet will not find filler on an aluminium car, so JDMBUYSELL advises an inspection by someone who knows NSXs. evo suggests looking for overspray in the nose and tail and checking that panel gaps are perfect.

Engine. The cambelt is the big service item. Car Throttle gives a 90,000-mile interval for the belt and water pump, while evo recommends every five years or 60,000 miles and advises replacing the camshaft pulley at the same time. Oil leaks are worth investigating: JDMBUYSELL, citing MotorTrend, lists the valve-cover gaskets, rear camshaft plugs and VTEC solenoids. evo says the engine should harden its note and surge as revs rise, a sign that VTEC and the variable intake are working.

Gearbox and clutch. Early five-speed gearboxes can have a faulty countershaft bearing snap ring, which can cause whining or jumping out of gear. evo lists the affected range as J4A4-1003542 to J4A4-1005978, and Car Throttle identifies it with 1991 and 1992 cars. Check that the fix has been done. Car Throttle also says the clutch master cylinder tends to leak at high mileage, so look for fluid under the dashboard.

Chassis and electrics. Car Throttle flags problems with the ABS control unit on early cars. evo warns that a worn front lower ball joint means replacing the whole upright, at about £1,000, and that rear tyres wear quickly. It also says the NSX-specific Bose stereo can cost about £1,000 to restore. UK specialist Plans Motorsport estimates maintenance at roughly £1,000 a year, according to evo.

How much is a Honda NSX worth today?

Values depend heavily on variant, mileage and originality, and the rare Japanese-market cars are a separate market. In the UK, evo's September 2025 guide put early cars from about £60,000 and the best low-mileage later cars at around £150,000. Car Throttle's 2024 survey of listings showed how wide the spread can be, with a high-mileage 1992 car advertised at $17,995 in the US, though that is an asking price rather than a typical sale.

At the top end, prices have climbed fast. The Drive reported in April 2025 that a dealer-supercharged 2002 Acura NSX-T with about 9,100 miles sold on Bring a Trailer for $370,000, the site's highest NSX price at the time, after a 2,000-mile 2005 NSX-T made $315,000 in December 2024. RM Sotheby's offered a 1994 NSX-R at its 2024 London sale with an estimate of £230,000 to £290,000, and it did not sell. On 31 October 2026, Ayrton Senna's own 1991 NSX sold at RM Sotheby's London for £871,250, about $1.17 million, which Motor1 reported as a world auction record for the model.
