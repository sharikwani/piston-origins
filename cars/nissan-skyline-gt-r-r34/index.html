Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 Buyer's Guide: Specs, Variants, Values

Updated 2026-11-09 · 9 sources

Nissan Skyline GT-R R34 at a glance

The R34 Skyline GT-R, chassis code BNR34, was revealed in January 1999 and built until 2002. It was the last GT-R to carry the Skyline name and the last to use the RB26DETT straight-six; its R35 successor dropped the Skyline badge. The episode page explains why the car was kept out of the US for so long. This page is for people who want to understand the variants, check a car before buying or put a value on one.

R34 GT-R specs

Every R34 GT-R used the 2,568 cc RB26DETT twin-turbo straight-six, officially rated at 280 PS (about 276 hp), the ceiling set by the Japanese makers' gentlemen's agreement. evo lists 276 bhp at 7,000 rpm and 289 lb ft at 4,400 rpm; PistonHeads gives the peak at 6,800 rpm. Both publications doubt the official figure: PistonHeads puts the real output nearer 327 hp, and evo says a simple ECU remap lifts it to around 350 bhp.

Drive went through a Getrag six-speed manual and Nissan's ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive system, with Super HICAS four-wheel steering. evo's spec table gives a kerb weight of 1,560 kg, a claimed 0-60 mph time of 4.8 seconds and a claimed top speed of 165 mph. The car is 4,600 mm long on a 2,665 mm wheelbase.

R34 GT-R variants: V-Spec, V-Spec II, M-Spec and Nür

The range was refreshed once, so buyers talk about Series 1 (1999 to 2000) and Series 2 (2000 to 2002) cars.

The standard GT-R was the base model. The V-Spec added the ATTESA E-TS Pro system with an active rear limited-slip differential, stiffer suspension, carbon rear diffusers and extra oil cooling, according to PistonHeads. In October 2000 the V-Spec was replaced by the V-Spec II, which added a carbon-fibre bonnet with a NACA duct; JDMBuySell dates its sale to 30 October 2000. The M-Spec arrived in May 2001 with softer "Ripple Control" dampers, leather seats and heated front seats, a more grown-up take on the car.

The Nür editions of 2002, named after the Nürburgring, used an engine based on the N1 racing specification, with gold cam covers and a 300 km/h speedometer. They were offered as the V-Spec II Nür and M-Spec Nür, often in Millennium Jade. Nissan announced a combined limit of 1,000 Nür cars across both models, as JDMBuySell points out, and the commonly quoted build figures are 718 V-Spec II Nür and 285 M-Spec Nür. There were also small numbers of N1 homologation cars with blueprinted engines.

The NISMO Z-tune is not a factory trim but a conversion of used GT-Rs with a 2.8-litre engine of nearly 500 bhp. NISMO says 19 were completed, though some sources say 20.

How many R34 GT-Rs were made?

The widely used total is 11,578 cars. PistonHeads' breakdown, based on chassis data, gives about 2,700 standard Series 1 cars and 4,300 V-Specs (including 38 N1s and the UK cars), then about 1,270 standard Series 2 cars, 1,850 V-Spec IIs, about 740 V-Spec II Nürs including 18 N1s, and 650 M-Specs, 285 of which were Nürs. Wikipedia's table gives slightly different splits, such as 718 V-Spec II Nürs and 366 M-Specs, so expect small variations between sources. JDMBuySell notes that Nissan's own published figures stop at the Nür limit and the end of production.

How the R34 GT-R was received in the UK

Unlike the earlier GT-Rs, the R34 was sold officially in Britain. Middlehurst made the V-Spec "Euro-proof" with extra oil coolers for the engine, gearbox and transfer box, a revised ECU and Connolly leather trim, and evo and PistonHeads both put UK sales at about 80 cars. PistonHeads quotes a UK price of £54,000; evo's road test gives £50,000 in 1999.

Retrospective reviews are warm. evo's John Barker, driving the UK press car in 2022, called it a genuine drivers' car, praised the gearshift, pedal spacing and "awe-inspiring" high-speed stability, and found the V-Spec's ride "firm but not rough", while noting that the steering is not the sharpest.

R34 GT-R in motorsport and pop culture

The R34 raced in the Japanese GT Championship. Nissan's Heritage Collection records the 1999 Pennzoil NISMO GT-R, a works car driven by Érik Comas and Satoshi Motoyama that scored points in all six rounds, with Comas winning the drivers' title two years running. Away from the track, Gran Turismo and 2 Fast 2 Furious made the R34 famous with people who had never seen one; the episode page covers the film car's $1.357 million sale.

R34 GT-R common problems: what to check

Most R34s have been modified, and many Japanese imports have lost their service history, so a specialist inspection is worth the money. PistonHeads' buying guide recommends a cold compression test, a look under the rocker cover, and checks for split vacuum lines, failing coil packs and failed air-flow meters. The cambelt should be changed every five years or 60,000 miles, and any ECU or wiring changes deserve scrutiny.

On the gearbox, PistonHeads and JDMBuySell both warn about the car jumping out of first gear. To check the four-wheel drive, PistonHeads suggests launching the car firmly and confirming that the multi-function display shows a 50/50 torque split; it also lists Super HICAS operation, front CV boots and front wheel bearings as things to inspect.

Rust is the other worry. Check the strut tops, rear arches, floorpan and the boot lid near the brake light, and look for overspray on rubber seals as a sign of accident repair. JDMBuySell adds that rebuilt cars and replacement instrument clusters complicate mileage checks, and that any Nür claim should be treated as unproven until the paperwork matches. The multi-function display screen can also fail.

How much is an R34 GT-R worth?

Prices rose sharply as the first cars became legal in the US in 2024, and the gap between ordinary and special cars is wide. In Japan, Logi-net's analysis of sales from March to May 2025 gives an average of about ¥18.1 million, with a range of ¥11.3 million to ¥35 million and rare variants approaching ¥30 million.

At the top end, a 2002 M-Spec Nür in Millennium Jade with about 60,000 km sold for $442,000 on Bring a Trailer on 25 January 2026, according to Automoto.it. Among the highest prices reported is the 10 km V-Spec II Nür that sold for ¥60,500,001 at BH Auction in Japan in 2021, as reported by Paultan. For context, PistonHeads put a typical UK car at around £100,000 in March 2020, with Nürs at £120,000.

JDMBuySell's figures from 9 November 2026 cover every R34 Skyline, including the far cheaper non-GT-R models, so they are not a guide to GT-R values; its median asking price for the whole family was US$35,620.

Importing an R34 GT-R into the US or UK

In the US, the 25-year exemption applies car by car from the month of manufacture: JDMBuySell notes that a January 1999 build became eligible in January 2024, and later cars follow month by month until the last 2002 builds qualify in 2027. The EPA generally expects the original engine configuration, and state rules vary. Early 2000s attempts to bring cars in through Motorex are covered on the episode page.

UK buyers can choose between the roughly 80 Middlehurst cars and Japanese imports. An import needs a declaration, VAT and duty, notification to HMRC within 14 days, vehicle approval where required, and DVLA registration; GOV.UK lists the steps.
