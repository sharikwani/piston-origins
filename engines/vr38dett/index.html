Nissan VR38DETT: The Hand-Built GT-R Engine, Specs and History

Updated 2026-11-09 · 6 sources

What is the VR38DETT?

The VR38DETT is the 3.8-litre, twin-turbocharged V6 that Nissan built for the R35 GT-R. The car was launched in 2007 and stayed in production until August 2025, when the last example left Nissan's Tochigi plant. Nissan says about 48,000 R35 GT-Rs were produced and sold over those 18 years, and every one of them used this engine.

The engine displaces 3,799 cc from a bore and stroke of 95.5 mm by 88.4 mm and runs a 9.0:1 compression ratio, according to the spec summary CarWhere compiled from Nissan's 2024 GT-R press kit. It has twin overhead camshafts per bank, four valves per cylinder, aluminium block and heads and two IHI turbochargers, with a maximum engine speed of 7,100 rpm.

At launch Nissan rated it at 480 PS (353 kW) at 6,400 rpm, with 588 Nm of torque from 3,200 to 5,200 rpm. By the end of production the standard GT-R made 570 PS and the GT-R Nismo up to 600 PS.

VR38DETT history: from 480 PS to 600 PS

The engine's output rose in steps over the car's life. Nissan's published US ratings, compiled by CarWhere, show 480 hp and 430 lb-ft for the 2009 model year, 485 hp for 2011, 530 hp for 2012 and 545 hp for 2013. For the 2012 car Nissan credited higher boost, revised valve timing and air-fuel ratio, and larger inlet and exhaust pipes.

The 2017 model year brought the most important update to the standard engine. MotoIQ reported 565 hp at 6,800 rpm and 467 lb-ft, gains of 20 hp and 4 lb-ft, achieved through more boost and an ignition-timing system controlled cylinder by cylinder, technology previously reserved for the GT-R Nismo. Nissan's own figure for this engine is 419 kW (570 PS). US and Japanese numbers differ because hp and PS are slightly different units.

The GT-R Nismo sat above that at 600 hp and 481 lb-ft. Nissan's April 2021 release for the GT-R Nismo Special Edition notes that it requires 93-octane fuel. According to CarWhere's summary of Nissan's 2021 pricing release, the Nismo received turbochargers derived from the GT-R GT3 race car from 2020, with a modified turbine shape and fewer blades than earlier units.

The engine's best-known performance milestones came on the Nürburgring Nordschleife. Nissan lists a 7:38 lap in 2007 and a 7:08.679 lap by the GT-R Nismo in 2013, the R35's best time there. Nissan also lists a production-car lap record at Japan's Tsukuba circuit, 59.361 seconds in 2019 and 59.078 seconds in 2024, and a win in the 2015 Bathurst 12 Hour among the car's racing results.

Takumi: how the VR38DETT is hand-built

The VR38DETT is built at Nissan's Yokohama engine plant, and its assembly is what sets it apart from most production engines. Nissan says each engine is hand-assembled from beginning to end in a special clean room by master technicians known as takumi. In its farewell release for the R35, Nissan says a core team of nine takumi assembled every engine over the car's life, and that their names appear on a plaque attached to each engine.

The plaque became part of the car's appeal. On the 2021 Nismo Special Edition, Nissan fitted the aluminium takumi certification plate in a colour exclusive to that model and used what it called high-precision, weight-balanced parts for the piston rings, connecting rods, crankshaft and valve gear, which it said give quicker revs and faster turbo response.

VR38DETT design details that matter

Instead of conventional cylinder liners, the VR38DETT has plasma-sprayed bores, a sprayed coating on the cylinder walls. An AZoM report from the car's 2008 launch also describes a special twin-turbo exhaust manifold system and a secondary air management system that helped the engine meet Japan's ultra-low emission vehicle standard. Mobil 1 0W-40 synthetic oil was the factory fill.

Lubrication is designed for hard driving. CarWhere, citing Nissan's 2009 launch release, describes a wet-and-dry-sump system with a scavenge pump for the turbochargers and a thermostatically controlled oil cooler. The engine sits at the front of the car and drives a GR6 six-speed dual-clutch transaxle mounted at the rear, with ATTESA E-TS all-wheel drive.

Cars fitted with the VR38DETT

In Nissan's production range the VR38DETT was built for the R35 GT-R. It powered every version of the car, from the standard coupé and Premium models to the Track Edition, T-spec and GT-R Nismo. The last car built was a Premium edition T-spec in Midnight Purple for a customer in Japan, which Nissan says was the model's final sales region. Nissan chief executive Ivan Espinosa said at the end of production that it was not a goodbye to the GT-R forever, but Nissan has not finalised a plan for the next car.

VR38DETT reputation, tuning potential and weak points

The engine has a strong reputation with tuners, and much of what is said about it comes from them rather than from Nissan. MotoIQ, in its Project DBA R35 GT-R series, called it "an exceedingly easy engine to get power out of" and set a target of 620 to 630 hp at the wheels using bolt-on parts alone. The same series noted that the R35 "is plagued by heat issues even in stock form", so its first modifications were aimed at cooling.

The limits of the standard car are usually reached outside the cylinder heads. MotoIQ reported that, at that power level, it was close to the limit of the standard connecting rods and driveline, and that further gains would need internal engine and gearbox work to stay reliable. Its stated end goal was 1,000 hp at the wheels, which it called "the GT-R's sweet spot". Treat figures like that as one workshop's view rather than a guaranteed result.

For standard engines, CarWhere's database records no NHTSA recalls for the VR38DETT.
