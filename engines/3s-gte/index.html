Toyota 3S-GTE Engine: Generations, Specs and History

Updated 2026-11-09 · 10 sources

What is the Toyota 3S-GTE?

The 3S-GTE is Toyota's 2.0-litre turbocharged four-cylinder engine, best known from the Celica GT-Four rally homologation cars, the second-generation MR2 Turbo and the Japanese-market Caldina estate. It belongs to the S family of four-cylinder engines and is the turbocharged version of the twin-cam 3S-GE.

Every version displaces 1,998 cc from a square bore and stroke of 86.0 mm by 86.0 mm, with twin overhead camshafts and four valves per cylinder. The block is cast iron and the cylinder head aluminium alloy. Output in Japan rose from 185 PS in 1986 to 260 PS by the time production ended in 2007.

3S-GTE history: from Camry engine to rally car

Motor1's history of the engine traces it to the 16-valve 3S-GE, which first appeared in a Japanese-market Camry in 1984. Motor1 notes that Toyota turned to its long-standing partner Yamaha for help with that higher-performance variant.

The turbocharged 3S-GTE arrived in October 1986 in the fourth-generation Celica GT-Four (ST165), a full-time four-wheel-drive model. Toyota's own history rates it at 185 PS with 24.5 kgm of torque, and evo calls it Japan's most powerful 2.0-litre engine of its day. In North America, where the car was sold as the All-Trac Turbo, Motor1 gives the output as around 190 hp.

The GT-Four was Toyota's way into Group A rallying after Group S was cancelled at the end of 1986. Motor1 records the car's World Rally Championship debut in 1988 with about 300 hp, its first win in 1989, Carlos Sainz's drivers' titles in 1990 and 1992, and both drivers' and manufacturers' titles in 1993 and 1994.

3S-GTE generations explained

Enthusiasts usually divide the engine into four or five generations. Those labels come from the owner community rather than from Toyota, and sources number them differently, so the summary below is organised by car as well as by generation.

The first generation powered the ST165 Celica GT-Four from 1986. JDMBUYSELL's engine reference, which cites Toyota's own catalogues, lists a water-cooled intercooler for this version.

The second generation arrived with the fifth-generation Celica GT-Four (ST185), released in September 1989, and the second-generation MR2 (SW20) launched in Japan the following month. Toyota rated it at 225 PS, 40 PS more than before. Motor1 credits the gain to a new intercooler and Toyota's twin-entry turbocharger, and JDMBUYSELL lists an air-cooled intercooler, an 8.8:1 compression ratio and 31.0 kgm of torque at 3,200 rpm. The Celica GT-Four RC, built for rallying, was rated at 235 PS with a water-cooled intercooler; Toyota says 5,000 RCs were released in 1992, while JDMBUYSELL dates its catalogue launch to September 1991. US-market MR2 Turbos were rated at 200 hp and 200 lb-ft, figures JDMBUYSELL attributes to Car and Driver.

The third generation appeared in the MR2 GT-S in October 1993 and in the sixth-generation Celica GT-Four (ST205) in February 1994. Japanese catalogue data for the MR2 lists 245 PS at 6,000 rpm, 31 kgm at 4,000 rpm and an 8.5:1 compression ratio. Toyota rates the ST205 at 255 PS at 6,000 rpm. Evo gives the European ST205 239 bhp and the Japanese car 251 bhp, the gap being mostly the difference between PS and bhp.

The final versions went into the Caldina estate. The second-generation Caldina GT-T (ST215W) arrived in 1997, and Toyota's catalogue for the January 2000 car lists 260 PS at 6,000 rpm, 33.0 kgm at 4,400 rpm and a 9.0:1 compression ratio. The third-generation Caldina GT-Four (ST246W), released in September 2002, kept 260 PS, though Toyota's history page lists the peak at 6,200 rpm. Production of that car ended in June 2007.

Cars fitted with the 3S-GTE

In summary, the road cars with the 3S-GTE were the Celica GT-Four in ST165, ST185 and ST205 form, including the RC and the ST205 WRC homologation model, the SW20 MR2 Turbo and its GT and GT-S grades, and the turbocharged Caldina GT-T and GT-Four. Toyota says 2,100 of the WRC-homologated ST205 GT-Four were released for the Japanese market in February 1994.

Motor1 also notes that the 3S-GTE was adapted for the Supra in Japan's GT500 racing class, and that Toyota's 503E racing engine was developed alongside the 3S family, though how much the two shared is unclear.

The 1995 turbo restrictor scandal

The ST205 is also the car at the centre of rallying's best-known engine controversy. Evo explains that the FIA had set a target of about 300 bhp for Group A and introduced an airflow restrictor in 1990, reduced over time from 40 mm to 38 mm and then 34 mm. At the 1995 Rally Catalunya, a device that bypassed the restrictor was found on a Toyota Team Europe car. The team was excluded from the 1995 season and banned from 1996.

3S-GTE reliability and known weaknesses

Evo describes the 3S-GTE as a durable unit, and the checks published guides recommend are mostly about maintenance. PistonHeads' Celica GT-Four buying guide recommends a cambelt change every 63,000 miles or five years, warns that ageing radiators can crack and leak, and says the water-to-air intercooler, sometimes called a charge cooler, needs its coolant checked for level and circulation. Smoke from the exhaust can point to a tired turbocharger or worse. JDMBUYSELL, citing Autocar, also flags cracking coolant pipes on ageing MR2s.

The engine never became well known in the United States. Motor1 says US sales were very limited and that Toyota did not recertify the engine for the 1996 OBD-II emissions rules.

How much power can a 3S-GTE make?

Estimates of how much power a standard engine will take vary, so they are best treated as opinions. PistonHeads says a healthy standard engine can handle about 300 bhp and 300 lb-ft, perhaps a little more with good supporting parts, but not substantial further gains without significant internal work. Evo gives a similar figure of about 300 bhp from reputable work and warns that tuned examples are more likely to fail.

Specialist tuners are more optimistic. Aaron Bunch of ATS Racing told Motor1 that the engine's connecting rods and bearings are about 25 per cent larger than the 4A-GE's, that 450 hp on standard internals is achievable, and that the most he has built reached 870 hp at the wheels.

What are 3S-GTE cars worth today?

Prices depend heavily on the model and condition, and published guides differ. PistonHeads, writing about the Celica GT-Four, said project cars could be found for as little as £3,000, sound cars cost upwards of £5,000 and the best reached around £10,000. Evo's guide to the ST205 suggests £15,000 to £20,000 for a usable higher-mileage UK car and up to £50,000 for a low-mileage, nearly perfect example.
